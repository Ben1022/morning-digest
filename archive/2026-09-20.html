The author proposes that package registries — the actual choke point where free code becomes corporate supply — start charging companies directly and pass funds to maintainers, since past voluntary approaches (tips, foundations, corporate pledges) have failed to scale.

Why it matters: A sharp analysis of the economics underpinning nearly all software (including AI tooling), with a concrete proposal to fix chronic open-source underfunding and security risk (e.g., the xz backdoor).

Key points
  • 60% of open-source maintainers are unpaid (Tidelift, 2021-2024); Harvard estimates open source is worth $8.8 trillion, with 5% of developers producing 96% of that value
  • Supply-chain companies (JFrog $532M, Snyk $326M, Docker $207M revenue) profit from selling dependable access to free code, while the maintainers who write it get nothing
  • Docker successfully charged large companies after 2020 rate-limiting/paywalling, proving companies will pay when billed as infrastructure rather than asked to donate
  • Past voluntary funding efforts (GitHub Sponsors, Flossbank, Ruby Together, corporate pledges) all failed to scale or collapsed due to free-riding

T. rex teeth suggest body temperature like an elephant's

Ars Technica Sun 2:00 AM 1 min read ●●●●●

UCLA researchers used clumped isotope thermometry on fossilized T. rex teeth to estimate its body temperature at about 36°C, comparable to a modern elephant. The method avoids a key flaw in earlier oxygen-isotope techniques by not depending on unknown ancient body-water composition.

Why it matters: An interesting science finding, though outside your core interests in tech and business.

Key points
  • Technique measures how often heavy carbon-13 and oxygen-18 isotopes bond in tooth enamel, which depends on formation temperature
  • Result supports the view that T. rex was warm-blooded (endothermic) rather than a sluggish reptile
  • Same method was previously applied to Jurassic sauropods by lead researcher Robert Eagle
  • Adds hard data to a long-running debate over dinosaur physiology previously based on indirect evidence
