Stratechery: AI labs' 'safety' slowdown push also solves business problems

Stratechery Mon 3:00 AM 15 min read ●●●●●

Ben Thompson argues that Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's push to 'pace the frontier' of AI development, framed as safety, conveniently addresses real business issues facing frontier labs: a price overhang (high prices sustained by compute going to training rather than inference), a capital overhang (Anthropic's claimed profitability excludes stock comp and training costs), and a capability overhang where 'good enough' models let rivals like Microsoft decouple harness from model, eroding differentiation. He contends cybersecurity risk actually argues for pushing the frontier faster, since automated attackers already have an inherent advantage over defenders who need even better AI to keep up.

Why it matters: Offers a contrarian, business-strategy lens on the AI safety debate just as Anthropic, OpenAI and others publicly call for slowdown and more regulation.

Key points
  • Microsoft's Satya Nadella confirmed Copilot now uses a multi-model harness (GPT, Anthropic, open-weight models), weakening any single lab's model-based moat
  • Anthropic reportedly told investors it will be profitable this quarter, but that excludes stock-based comp and massive training costs (per FT)
  • Thompson: 'pacing the frontier' would let labs reallocate compute from training to inference, effectively preserving high prices
  • Argues AI cyber-defense risk favors faster frontier progress, not slower, since offensive automated attacks already have positive expected value
